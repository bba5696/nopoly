Who can play

You must be at least 13 years old to use nopoly. If you are under 18, you should have a parent or guardian's permission. We don't verify anyone's age — this is a rule, not a gate — but if we become aware that someone under 13 is using the game, we will remove them and delete anything of theirs still held.

Acceptable use

The game has a chat box and lets you choose your own display name. Please don't use either to:

  • harass, threaten, bully or abuse anyone;
  • post hateful content targeting people for who they are;
  • post sexual content involving minors, or anything otherwise illegal;
  • impersonate someone else in a way meant to deceive;
  • share other people's personal information;
  • spam, flood, or deliberately disrupt other people's games; or
  • attack, overload, or attempt to break into the server.

There is no moderation team. nopoly is run by one person who is not watching your game. The tools that exist are the ones in the game itself: rooms are private and joined with a 5-character code you have to be given, and a table can vote out someone the turn clock has had to keep playing for, or who has left. Who called that vote and how everyone voted is written into the room's history feed. Beyond that, we may remove content, end a game, or block access at our discretion and without notice — but we are under no obligation to monitor anything, and we don't.

You are responsible for what you type. Anything you send in chat is visible to everyone else in that room, including spectators.

Availability, and the lack of it

nopoly runs on a single free-tier virtual machine. It is provided as is, with no promise that it works, stays up, or keeps your game. Specifically, and by design:

  • Games are held in the server's memory. A restart, a crash, or a deploy ends them, apart from a short-lived snapshot described under retention.
  • The game may be changed, broken, or shut down permanently at any time, without notice or announcement.
  • There is no support, no service level, and no backup you can ask to be restored from.

It is a game between friends. Please treat it as one.

Privacy Policy

What nopoly collects, which is very little, and what happens to it.

The honest summary: nopoly was built for a private group of friends, so it never needed to know who anybody is. There are no accounts, no email addresses, no profiles, and no database. Nothing is sold, shared, or used for advertising, because there is no advertising.

The game loads nothing from third-party servers. Fonts are bundled into the app, country flags are stored locally in the repository, and there are no analytics scripts, tag managers, embedded widgets, or social buttons. When you open nopoly, your browser talks to the nopoly server and to nothing else.

What the server receives

WhatWhyWhere it lives
Display name, initials and colour So the table knows who is who. Whatever you type — a real name is neither asked for nor needed. Server memory, for the life of the game
Chat messages and game actions To play the game and show the room's history feed Server memory, for the life of the game
A random browser ID nopoly.client Only to tell two tabs of one person apart when counting who's online. Not a login and not tied to any identity. Your browser, plus server memory while connected
Your IP address Unavoidable — every web request carries one. Used only to rate-limit password guesses, and only on deployments that set a password. Server memory, and the web server's access log

That is the complete list. There is no tracking of what you click, how long you stay, where you came from, or what device you use. No profile of you is built, and nothing is linked across sessions beyond the random browser ID above.

Stored in your browser

nopoly sets no cookies. It uses your browser's local storage for five values, all of which are strictly necessary for the game to work, and none of which are ever sent anywhere except to the nopoly server:

KeyWhat it holds
nopoly.tokenYour access token, if the site has a password set
nopoly.identityYour nickname, initials, colour and current room, so a refresh puts you back in your seat
nopoly.clientThe random browser ID described above
nopoly:mutedWhether you muted the sound
nopoly:musicWhether you turned the music on

Because none of these are used for tracking or advertising, there is no consent banner — there is nothing to consent to. You can clear all of them at any time through your browser's site-data settings, which resets you to a first-time visitor.

How long anything is kept

  • Live games — held in the server's memory only, and never written to a database. A restart ends them. So does the server's own cleanup, which discards a room 30 minutes after the last player disconnects, 30 minutes after the game finishes, or after 3 hours in which nobody in it has done anything — whichever comes first. Everything in that room, chat included, goes with it.
  • The crash snapshot — every 15 seconds, and on shutdown, active games are written to a file on the server so a restart doesn't end everyone's evening. It contains player names, chat and board state. It is deleted the moment it is restored, and ignored and discarded if it is more than 6 hours old.
  • Failed password attempts — an IP address is held in memory for up to 10 minutes after a wrong password, purely to apply the cooldown, then dropped.
  • Web server access logs — standard request logs, kept short-term for security and debugging, then rotated away.

Nothing is archived. There is no long-term store, so there is nothing to accumulate.

Who it is shared with

Nobody. Your data is not sold, rented, traded or given to advertisers, analytics providers or anyone else. The only parties involved in running the game are the hosting provider whose machine it runs on, and, for people reaching the site through its alternate address, a content delivery network that forwards the traffic. Neither is given anything beyond what is technically required to deliver the page.

We would disclose information only if legally required to — and given the above, there would be almost nothing to disclose.

Your rights

Most privacy rights — access, correction, deletion — you can exercise yourself, instantly, without asking:

  • See what's held about you: your nickname and colour, which are shown on screen, and the browser values listed above.
  • Correct it: change your nickname in the game.
  • Delete it: leave the room and clear the site's data in your browser. Server-side, your data disappears when the game ends.

If you want a specific game's snapshot deleted sooner, or you have a question or complaint about privacy, get in touch and we'll deal with it — realistically, by deleting the snapshot file, which takes about ten seconds.

As a non-commercial project run by an individual, nopoly falls under the small business exemption in the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth) and is not strictly bound by the Australian Privacy Principles. We follow them anyway, since collecting almost nothing makes that easy. If you are in the EU or UK, the same applies to the GDPR: our lawful basis for the little we process is legitimate interest in running the game and keeping it secure, and you can exercise your rights through the contact below.

Children

nopoly is not directed at children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect anything from them. Since we collect no identifying information from anyone, there is very little that could be collected — but if you believe a child under 13 has used the game and something of theirs is held, contact us and it will be removed.

Security

Traffic is encrypted with HTTPS. Where a password is set, it is never stored in your browser — only a token derived from it — and all tokens stop working the moment the password changes. That said, no system is perfectly secure, and this one is a hobby project on a free server. Please don't put anything sensitive into it.
